## Telangana State Curriculum Framework (SCF) 2011 [S-official, summary]

Source: SCF-2011 PDF on scert.telangana.gov.in (link in SOURCES.md). A Scribd copy calls itself
the Andhra Pradesh framework (the state split in 2014), so the version should be checked.

- **Mother tongue / home language.** Quoted in the summary: a child arrives at school "having
  already mastered the spoken forms of the languages of her neighbourhood", and the "medium of
  instruction shall, as far as practicable, be in child's mother tongue".
- **Learning process (constructivist).** Quoted: "Teaching-learning processes consist of
  explorations, experiments, discovery, activities, tasks, project work etc." Related Telangana
  teacher-education material describes the shift from behaviourism to constructivism and social
  constructivism.
- **Position papers.** The Telangana D.El.Ed syllabus cites "State Curriculum Framework (SCF) 2011 –
  Position Paper on Curriculum, Syllabus and Textbooks"; its language section covers First
  Language (mother tongue), Second and Third Language. [S-secondary]
- **Role of the teacher.** [GAP] Not reached. (A non-Telangana journal article describes the
  constructivist teacher as "facilitator, co-learner, democratic leader and diagnostician"; this
  is NCF-2005 commentary, not SCF.)
- **Inclusion.** [GAP]

## English language pedagogy in Telangana [S-official/secondary, summary]

- SCERT's 2018 English secondary training module organises a unit's components "as listening,
  speaking (oral activity), reading and writing, an integrated treatment of these skills has been
  worked out", and has a training session on "analysis of discourses" (official URL).
- Telangana's learning outcomes say the aim is to acquire "the skills of listening, speaking,
  reading, writing and thinking in an integrated manner" [S-secondary].
- English **academic standards** (as listed by a teacher site [S-secondary]): Listening and
  speaking; Reading comprehension; Conventions of writing; Vocabulary and grammar; Creative
  expression (oral and written).
- Progression in primary English (alignment-sheet guide [S-secondary]): Class 1 oral language
  development, picture interaction, alphabet readiness → Class 5 reading comprehension, grammar,
  creative writing, conversations, editing, project work.

## Tholimettu (First Step), Telangana's FLN mission [S-secondary]

- Launched for Classes 1-5 in 2022-23 "to improve the foundational outcomes", developed with
  Central Square Foundation; continued in 2025-26.
- SCERT gives teachers model **annual, unit/lesson and daily period plans**: "a month-wise,
  week-wise, and day-wise annual plan for classes 1 to 5, along with detailed period plans". Each
  unit runs roughly 10-15 periods; each bridge activity about 5.
- Reading targets cited: "20 words per minute in class 1, 25 words … per minute in class 2".
- Writing targets cited: Class 1 simple words; Class 2 words with diacritics ("accented words");
  Classes 3-5 paragraphs of 4-5 sentences.
- Foundational numeracy components: pre-number concepts, numbers and operations, shapes and
  spatial understanding, measurement, data handling.

## National frame

- **NIPUN Bharat** (launched July 2021) [S-official PIB doc + secondary]: every child reaches
  foundational literacy and numeracy by the end of Grade 3, by 2026-27. Targets ("Lakshyas") run
  Balvatika → Grade 3 and are "based on NCERT learning outcomes and international research and ORF
  studies". Oral reading fluency: Grade 2 about 45-60 wcpm, Grade 3 at least 60 wcpm, on an
  unfamiliar age-appropriate text with comprehension. Balvatika targets are qualitative (recognise
  letters and sounds, read simple 2-3-letter words; count and read numerals to 10). Grade 1 figure:
  [GAP] (garbled in the summary). Note Tholimettu's own Class 1-2 figures (20/25 wpm) are lower
  than NIPUN's Grade 2 figure.
- **NCF for the Foundational Stage 2022** (launched 20 Oct 2022) [secondary]: ages 3-8;
  *panchakosha* model of development (physical, life-energy, emotional-mental, intellectual,
  spiritual); play at the core of curriculum organisation; aimed at FLN for all. Home language and
  observation-based assessment wording: [GAP].
- **NCERT Learning Outcomes at the Elementary Stage (2017)** [S-official, summary]: per class and
  subject, a "Suggested Pedagogical Processes" column beside a "Learning Outcomes" column, each
  introduced by "The learner:". Class I English examples quoted: "name common objects like man, dog
  etc. when pictures are shown"; "Recites poems and rhymes along with actions" (state-circulated
  version). NCERT's 2017 document has no codes; states added their own (e.g. Meghalaya
  `1.E.LO1`). Telangana's codes: [GAP].
- **NEP 2020**: not reached by search in this session [GAP].

## What this means for the app (inference, not source)

- The documents found consistently ask for **integrated skills** (listen → speak → read → write)
  in one lesson, not drills in isolation; **picture interaction** before text; and assessment that
  is **continuous** (FA tools: observation, written work, project, slip test — see 03).
- Mother-tongue first: Telugu content and Telugu UI for Classes 1-2 are on-policy.
- ORF in wpm is the government's own reading metric (Tholimettu 20/25; NIPUN 45-60/60), which
  matches the app's existing `orfTargetWpm`; the Class 1-2 values may need to follow Tholimettu.
